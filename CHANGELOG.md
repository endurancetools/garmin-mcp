# Changelog

All notable changes to Garmin MCP. All `garmin-mcp-*` packages share one version. The same list is on the [documentation site](https://endurancetools.github.io/garmin-mcp/changelog/).

## Unreleased

### Tools

- **Solar input in `get_devices`.** A device that charges from the sun, such as a Solar watch, now has `solar`: one row per day of the last 7 days with the date, `time_gained_ms` (the battery time Garmin says the sun added that day, in milliseconds as Garmin reports it), `solar_minutes` (the minutes with any solar input) and `peak_solar_utilization` (the day's highest reading, under Garmin's name, as Garmin does not say its unit). In autumn the time gained is often 0 even on a sunny day, so the minutes and the peak show what the day brought. The days are downloaded with the device settings when the device list is first fetched, and after that `get_devices` refreshes them by itself once they are older than an hour, with auto fetch on (`GARMIN_AUTO_FETCH`): one request per solar device within the daily request limit, without downloading the device list and settings again. Garmin's minute-by-minute readings are summed up and not kept. When the daily limit is used up, syncing is paused or the request fails, the days already downloaded stay, and the answer's note says when they were downloaded; a device whose days were never downloaded is listed without `solar`. With auto fetch off the days change only with a `garmin_sync` of the reference scope. Other devices have no `solar`.
- **The training plan and Garmin's threshold history follow auto fetch.** `list_workouts` downloads the active training plan and the analytics tools download Garmin's threshold history whenever auto fetch is on (`GARMIN_AUTO_FETCH`, on by default), as courses and hydration do; before, they were downloaded only in a mode the server never runs in, so the `plan` block, the plan's line for each workout and the `garmin-auto` thresholds from Garmin's history stayed empty. With auto fetch off neither is downloaded, and `garmin_sync` does not fetch them.
- **The course of a race in `list_workouts`.** An event in `upcoming_events` that has a course assigned in Garmin Connect now carries `course`: its id, name, distance and elevation gain. The figures come from your course list, downloaded by `list_workouts` itself when an event has a course (one request within the daily request limit, kept for an hour); a course that is not in your list, such as one the organiser shared, is read from its own details. When the course cannot be downloaded, the event keeps the course id and name from the calendar. The course of a calendar event follows the shape of the recorded calendar items and is not confirmed on a race with a course yet.
- **Hydration in `query_health`.** The new metric `hydration` gives the water you logged per day in ml, your daily goal and the sweat loss Garmin estimates from your activities, so "did I drink enough this week" has an answer; per week, the averages. A day without a water entry is left out rather than shown as 0, and when nothing is logged in the range the answer says so. Hydration is downloaded by the question itself, one request per day within the daily request limit, and kept: today's after 15 minutes again, a past day for good; `garmin_sync` does not fetch it. `garmin health hydration` shows what is cached.
- **Threshold history from Garmin.** `get_thresholds` now has Garmin's own history of your running lactate threshold, heart rate and pace, and the date Garmin set your cycling FTP: the analytics tools download it once a day (three requests within the daily request limit, the last 365 days; `garmin_sync` does not) and record each change as a `garmin-auto` row with Garmin's date, so `run_threshold_pace_s_per_km` and `ftp_w` appear with `source: garmin-auto` and `history: true` shows when your threshold moved. A value Garmin repeats is recorded once, your own `test`, `manual` and `import` rows are never changed, and a test on the same day as a Garmin change keeps precedence. Backdated rows recompute `get_load` for the activities from their date on, as a backdated `set_threshold` does. How it works: [Training analytics](https://endurancetools.github.io/garmin-mcp/analytics/#thresholds-with-history).
- **Typed splits of an activity.** `get_activity` with `detail: "splits"` (and `"full"`) now adds `typed_splits` next to the laps: the stretches Garmin marks as running, walking or standing, ClimbPro climbs and the other split types it records, in start order, each with the local start time, time, distance, gain and loss, average and steepest grade (climbs), pace and grade adjusted pace (speed for other sports) and heart rate, so "how long did I walk and how long did I run on Saturday's trail" has an answer. `split_summaries` adds the totals per type: how many, time, distance, gain and loss, pace, GAP and heart rate. ClimbPro also cuts every climb into sections; a long trail run has hundreds of them, so they are left out of the list. The run, walk and stand stretches are often only seconds long, so one shorter than two minutes is merged into the run, walk or stand stretch before it (at the start, into the next one): times, distances, gain and loss add up, heart rate and GAP are averaged over time, and the merged stretch takes the type of the longer part. The answer's note says how many were merged; `split_summaries` keep Garmin's exact totals and counts. The typed splits are one more request per activity, made once with the laps and kept for good; the totals come with the activity itself, so they cost a request only when it lacks them. An activity without typed splits leaves both lists out.
- **Training plan workouts in `list_workouts`.** Workouts of a Garmin Coach plan (or another training plan) were left out of the calendar; `upcoming_schedule` now lists them next to the ones scheduled from your library, each row with `source` (`library` or `plan`) and, for a plan workout, the plan's name, the sport and, for the current week, one line from the plan such as "50 min, 9.7 km, tempo, key workout" (Garmin lists the tasks of one week ahead only). A new `plan` block describes the active plan: name, adaptive or phased, sport, start and end, the current phase (base, build, peak, taper) and the target event, with the goal race's title and distance when it is on your calendar. The plan is downloaded by `list_workouts` itself when the calendar has its workouts, one request within the daily request limit, and kept for an hour. Plan workouts have no steps in your library, so `get_workout` cannot show them, and `schedule_workouts` refuses to unschedule them with an error that says so: the plan is changed in Garmin Connect or on the watch, not here. Garmin Coach plans follow a recording; phased plans are not confirmed yet.
- **Add a manual activity.** `update_activity` without an activity `id` adds one by hand, for the yoga class or swim the watch did not record: `type` (an activity type key such as `yoga`), `start` as a local time (`2026-11-16T18:30`), `duration_min`, and optionally `distance_km`, `name` (else the type, e.g. "Yoga"), `description`, `rpe`, `feel` and `privacy`. The activity is private unless you choose otherwise. Calories are left for Garmin to work out from the type, the duration and your profile. It is in the local cache at once, so `get_activities` lists it without a sync. The assistant shows you the values first and adds the activity only after you agree; as every call adds another activity, the tool is no longer marked idempotent. Fields of the other kind are refused before anything is written: `start`, `duration_min` and `distance_km` with an `id`, gear, event type and strength sets without one; a start in the future is refused too. The tool's title is now "Edit or add an activity"; its name stays `update_activity`, so tool allow lists and client permissions keep working.
- **Activity privacy and strength sets.** `update_activity` takes `privacy` (`public`, `followers` for your connections, or `private`) and `exercise_sets`, the strength sets of the activity: per set the exercise as Garmin's category and exercise keys (e.g. `SQUAT` / `BARBELL_BACK_SQUAT`), reps, weight in kg, duration and the rest after it. The sets replace all sets of the activity, so the assistant shows you the new sets and saves them only after you agree; the answer has the sets before the change in `previous_exercise_sets`, in the same shape, so they can be written back. With `keep_timing: true` the times the watch recorded stay and only the exercise, reps and weight change (one entry per recorded set, a field left out keeps its value): the usual fix when the watch recognised the wrong exercise. An exercise key that is not in Garmin's catalogue is refused before anything is written, naming the set and the field. Replacing the sets drops the ones the watch recorded, so `exercise_sets` and `keep_timing` are there only with `GARMIN_ENABLE_DESTRUCTIVE=true`, like overwriting a saved workout, and the tool is then marked destructive; without it sending them is an error that names the setting. `privacy` and the other edits work without it.
- **Power curve of an activity.** `get_activity` with `detail: "power_curve"` gives the best average power for 1, 5, 15 and 30 seconds and 1, 2, 5, 10, 20, 30 and 60 minutes, in watts and W/kg (weight from your latest weigh-in up to 30 days before the activity, else from your Garmin profile; the note says which), with the time from the start where each best effort began. Running power counts the same way. It is computed from the activity's FIT file: the file is downloaded once (one request within the daily request limit), the curve is kept in the local cache and the file is not. Seconds without power, such as stops, count as 0 W, so the best hour is not joined across a café stop. An activity without power answers `power_curve: null` without downloading anything, and `detail: "full"` does not download the file. How it is computed: [Training analytics](https://endurancetools.github.io/garmin-mcp/analytics/#power-curve).
- **Best power in comparisons and an FTP estimate.** `compare_activities` adds `best_5min_w` and `best_20min_w` to each activity whose power curve is already stored (by `get_activity` with `detail: "power_curve"`); comparing never downloads FIT files, so a ride with power but no stored curve leaves them out and one note says so. `get_thresholds` adds `estimates.ftp_w`: 95 % of the best 20 minute power of your rides in the 90 days up to `date` with a stored curve, with the ride's date and id. It is an estimate the server shows and never records: your FTP threshold and `get_load` stay as they are until you agree to record it with `set_threshold`. How it is computed: [Training analytics](https://endurancetools.github.io/garmin-mcp/analytics/#ftp-estimate).
- **A FIT file with a broken clock cannot stall the server.** When `garmin-mcp-fit` reads an activity file, a record or gear shift whose timestamp lies far outside the activity (a device clock reset to 1989, a jump years ahead) is left out instead of stretching the second-by-second series over decades: the activity is the span of the file's session, or, without a usable one, the largest run of records less than 12 hours apart. A file whose records still span more than 7 days is refused with a clear message; `get_activity` with `detail: "power_curve"` then says the file could not be read and keeps nothing, so the next question tries again.
- **Climbs of an activity.** `get_activity` with `detail: "stream_summary"` and `bucket: "climbs"` gives one row per climb: where it starts, length, gain, average and steepest grade, pace and grade adjusted pace (speed for other sports), heart rate and power, VAM (metres climbed per hour) and, with power, W/kg from your latest weigh-in up to 30 days before the activity or the weight in your Garmin profile. A climb is at least 500 m long, 3 % steep on average and 30 m high; flatter bits up to 200 m long do not split it. It is computed from the stream `stream_summary` already downloads, with no extra request; an activity without climbs gives an empty list and says so. How climbs are found: [Training analytics](https://endurancetools.github.io/garmin-mcp/analytics/#climbs).
- **`query_health` answers per hour.** With `granularity: "hour"` (up to 3 days) it gives a row per hour: heart rate (average, lowest, highest), stress (average, highest), Body Battery at the start and end of the hour, steps, respiration and SpO2, and per metric the average, the peak and the lowest hour, so "when was I most stressed yesterday" has an answer. Asked about stress or Body Battery, it also lists the Body Battery events (sleep, nap, activity, stress) with start, duration and impact, for "what drained my Body Battery". Sleep, HRV, calories, intensity minutes and floors have no hourly values and come per day next to the hours. These series are downloaded only by an hourly question, one request per series and day within the daily request limit, and need auto fetch (`GARMIN_AUTO_FETCH`, on by default); `garmin_sync` does not download them. A past day is kept for good, today's is refreshed after 15 minutes.
- **`get_sleep` shows how one night went.** For a single night (`date`) the answer has a `timeline`: the sleep stages as segments with start and end (at most 60, short ones merged into their neighbours), awakenings (how many, the longest and when each began), restless moments, the lowest heart rate, the lowest and highest HRV reading and the lowest SpO2 with their times, and Body Battery at the start and end of the night. Clock times are in your time zone. It is read from the sleep and HRV data already downloaded for that night, so it costs no extra requests to Garmin; ranges of nights are unchanged.

### Resources and prompts

- **Badges and challenges as a resource.** `garmin://challenges` gives how many badges you earned and the 10 latest (name, date, points and, for a badge earned more than once, how many times), and the badge challenges you joined that are still in progress, each with its start and end date and progress in %, so "how far am I in this month's challenge" has an answer. The value and target of a challenge stay in Garmin's unit (`unit_id`), as Garmin does not name it. Both lists are downloaded when read, one request each within the daily request limit and only with auto fetch on, and kept for an hour; `garmin_sync` does not fetch them. Ad-hoc challenges between connections, challenges you could still join and virtual challenges are not included. The tool list is unchanged.
- **Courses as resources.** `garmin://courses` lists the courses saved in Garmin Connect, newest first: id, name, sport, distance and elevation gain. `garmin://course/{id}` (with id completion from the list) describes one course: distance, elevation gain and loss, the lowest and highest point, the profile in 20 stretches of equal length (elevation, gain, loss and grade of each) and the climbs found the same way as `bucket: "climbs"` of an activity (at least 500 m long, 3 % steep on average and 30 m high), so "how hilly is this course and where are the climbs" has an answer before the race. Positions (the start and the start of each stretch) are there only with `GARMIN_REDACT=false`. Courses are downloaded when read, one request each within the daily request limit and only with auto fetch on, and kept for an hour; `garmin_sync` does not fetch them.
- **`garmin://today`, the morning at a glance.** A new resource with today's GO / MODIFY / REST decision from `get_readiness` (default rules) with the rating of each signal and the advice, last night in one line (start and end in your time zone, duration, score, deep, REM and awake time, awakenings, HRV and resting heart rate) and the workouts on today's calendar. It is read from the local cache only and never downloads anything, not even with auto fetch on, so a client can attach it to every conversation; when days are missing, its note names the tool that downloads them (`get_readiness`, `get_sleep`, `list_workouts`).
- **Three new prompts.** `sleep_report` reads a range of nights (default the last 7), then the worst night hour by hour from its timeline. `activity_review` reviews one activity (default the most recent) with its laps and sets it against your similar sessions with `compare_activities`. `race_check` finds the next race on your calendar and checks your form (`get_load`) and Garmin's race predictions (`get_training_status`) against the days left. The Claude Desktop extension lists all five prompts. Resources and prompts are not part of the tool list, so it stays the same size.

### CLI

- **`garmin course` downloads and uploads courses.** `garmin course download <id>` saves a course as a GPX file, in the current directory or at `--out` (a file, or a directory for the name Garmin gives). `garmin course upload <file.gpx>` adds a GPX file as a new course and prints its id, name, distance and gain; without a name in the file the course is named after it. Both take `--json`. They follow the known shape of the Garmin Connect API and are not confirmed on a real account yet. Deleting a course is not offered.
- **`garmin upload` and `garmin log` write to Garmin Connect.** `garmin upload <file...>` sends FIT, GPX or TCX files as new activities and prints each new activity's id; the activity goes into the local database at once, so it is there without a sync. A file Garmin already has is reported as a duplicate, with the existing activity's id when Garmin gives it, and does not fail the command; a file Garmin refuses is reported and the other files are still sent. `garmin log weight 72.4`, `garmin log bp 120/80 --pulse 62` and `garmin log water 500` add a weigh-in, a blood pressure reading or water to Garmin Connect, now or at `--date` (`YYYY-MM-DD` or `"YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM"` in your time zone; a past day without a time is 12:00; nothing in the future). After a weigh-in that day's weigh-ins are downloaded again, so `get_body_composition` shows it. All of them take `--json`. The upload and the blood pressure and water writes follow the known shape of the Garmin Connect API and are not confirmed on a real account yet. The MCP tools still do not write measurements.
- **`garmin log weight --fat` writes body fat.** `garmin log weight 72.4 --fat 18.5` adds the weigh-in with body fat in %. Garmin Connect takes body composition only as a file from a scale, so the command writes a small weight scale FIT file and sends it through the upload service; without `--fat` the weight goes as a plain weigh-in, as before. That day's weigh-ins are downloaded again afterwards, so `get_body_composition` shows the value; when Garmin answers that it is still processing the file, the command says so and how to download the day again later. A file Garmin refuses fails the command and adds nothing. Body fat from 1 to 75 %. Not confirmed on a real account yet.
- **`garmin record` knows 20 more endpoints**, the ones the next features read: typed splits and split summaries of an activity, training plans (list, phased, adaptive), lactate threshold history (heart rate and speed), cycling FTP, hydration, blood pressure, the day's events, goals, courses, a device's solar gain, nutrition, the menstrual cycle day, earned badges, badge challenges in progress and virtual challenges in progress (`virtual-challenges`). `garmin record list` shows them. Their paths follow the known shape of the Garmin Connect API and are not confirmed by a recording yet. Recordings of training plans, courses, goals and daily events replace every name, as plan, race, course point and goal names name places; course coordinates are shifted like activity tracks, and encoded polylines are replaced. As nobody has seen these responses yet, their recordings replace every text value except enum-like constants (`RUNNING`), dates, timestamps and type and unit names, so notes, foods or symptoms under unknown keys stay out of fixtures. Notes, comments and food, meal and brand names are replaced in every recording. After writing the file, `garmin record` lists the keys where text is left, to review before committing.

### Local cache

- **Solar days are kept.** The local database moves to schema version 13 so that the daily solar sums of a device can be stored with it. The upgrade keeps everything you have; the device list is downloaded again on the next refresh, this time with the solar days.
- **Hydration is kept.** The local database moves to schema version 12 so that the day's hydration can be stored in the `intraday` table next to the intraday series. The upgrade keeps everything you have.
- **Typed splits are kept.** The local database moves to schema version 11 so that the typed splits of an activity and their totals per type can be stored next to its laps. The upgrade keeps everything you have.
- **Training plan workouts are kept.** The local database moves to schema version 10: calendar workouts carry their source, the library or a training plan. The upgrade marks the workouts you have as library ones and drops the list of cached calendar months, so the next `list_workouts` fetches them again with the plan workouts.
- **Power curves are kept.** The local database moves to schema version 9 so that the power curve of an activity can be stored next to its other details. The upgrade keeps everything you have.
- **Room for intraday data.** The local database (schema version 8) gets an `intraday` table for one day's heart rate, stress with the Body Battery curve, respiration, SpO2, steps, Body Battery events and the day's events. Hourly `query_health` fills it: these series are downloaded only by the question that needs them, never by `garmin_sync`. Today's series are refreshed after 15 minutes and a past day is kept for good; every request counts against the daily request limit (`GARMIN_SYNC_MAX_REQUESTS_PER_DAY`) shared with syncing, and no download starts while syncing is paused after Garmin refused requests. The upgrade only adds the table; the data you have stays.

## 0.6.3 (2026-11-17)

### Tools

- **Clearer `list_workouts`, `download_activity_file` and `get_sleep`.** Each now names the tool to use instead: `get_workout` for the steps of one workout, `schedule_workouts` for the calendar and `get_activities` for sessions already done; `get_activity` when the assistant should read an activity rather than save it as a file. `list_workouts` says that `sport` and `limit` narrow only the library and `from`/`to` only the calendar. `download_activity_file` says how the file is named and that it replaces an earlier download of the same activity and format. `get_sleep` says that a night is named by the date of the morning it ended on, that a range can span up to 3 years, and that missing nights are downloaded within the daily request limit.

## 0.6.2 (2026-11-17)

### Tools

- **Shorter tool descriptions, same guidance.** Text that said the same thing twice (in a tool's description and again on its parameter), implementation details that do not change which tool fits, and example calls for tools with flat inputs are gone; "when to use it" and "which tool instead" stay. The tool list the assistant reads with every request is about 4 % smaller.
- **Tool input schemas no longer carry `$schema`.** MCP reads a schema that names no dialect as JSON Schema 2020-12, which is what the server emits, so the key only took up context in every tool definition. With the shorter descriptions the tool list is now 36,371 bytes (about 9,100 tokens), 7 % less than in 0.6.1.

## 0.6.1 (2026-11-17)

### Tools

- **Fuller descriptions of `get_workout`, `get_gear` and `get_thresholds`.** Each now says what it returns, when to use it and which tool it pairs with (`get_workout` takes the id from `list_workouts` or the `workout_id` of `get_activity`; the name or uuid from `get_gear` is what `update_activity` takes as `add_gear`/`remove_gear`; `get_thresholds` shows what `get_load` and `get_readiness` compute with, `set_threshold` records a new test). All their parameters have descriptions, so the assistant picks the right tool and arguments more often.
- **Clearer descriptions for the other tools too.** `get_sleep`, `get_body_composition`, `get_training_status`, `get_devices` and `compare_activities` now say when to use them and which tool to use instead (e.g. `get_sleep` for the stages of particular nights, `query_health` for sleep hours next to other metrics; `get_readiness` for a go/no-go today, `get_training_status` for VO2max and race predictions). They also say the default range, that ranges include both ends, and what comes back when there is no data. Parameters without a description in `get_activities`, `list_workouts`, `update_activity`, `download_activity_file`, `compare_activities` and `set_threshold` have one now, e.g. the `duration_pct` and `pace_s_per_km` tolerances of `similar_to` and the `test`/`manual` sources of `set_threshold`. `get_load` is split up: its description says what the model is and when to use it, and each `include_*` option describes what it adds. The tool list the assistant reads with every request is about 8 % larger.

## 0.6.0 (2026-11-17)

### Signing in

- **Sign in from the assistant, without the terminal.** When a tool needs Garmin and nobody is signed in, the MCP server now opens Garmin's own sign-in page in your browser by itself: the same window `garmin login` opens, so the password still never passes through the tool. The tool call waits up to 30 s for you. When you need longer it answers `AUTH_SIGN_IN_PENDING`, the window stays open, and the next call goes on once you are signed in. The window also comes back when Garmin no longer accepts the stored tokens. This works where the server runs on your own computer (stdio) with Chrome, Edge, Brave or Chromium installed; a server in Docker or reached over HTTP reports `AUTH_NOT_LOGGED_IN` with the `garmin login` hint, as before. `GARMIN_AUTO_LOGIN=false` (in the Claude Desktop extension: **Open Garmin's sign-in page automatically**) turns it off.
- **A sign-in that Garmin refuses says so.** When Garmin's sign-in API answers its own page with 403 or 429, the page shows an error whatever the password, and when the page itself is blocked it never gets past "Just a moment...". The failed sign-in now names that status and says to wait or to change the network, instead of only "the browser window was closed".
- **Syncing goes on right after a new sign-in.** A sync that found nobody signed in pauses all syncing for an hour, and signing in again did not end that pause. `garmin login`, `garmin import-tokens` and the sign-in from the assistant now end it; a pause for a rate limit or a Cloudflare block stays.

### Activities

- **Tag an activity as a race.** `update_activity` takes `event_type`: `race`, `training`, `recreation`, `special_event`, `fitness`, `touring`, `transportation`, `geocaching` or `uncategorized`. `get_activities` and `get_activity` show `event_type` when an activity has one other than `uncategorized`, and `get_activities` filters by it, so "show all my races this year" is one call. ([#3](https://github.com/endurancetools/garmin-mcp/issues/3))
- **Gear on activities.** `update_activity` takes `add_gear` and `remove_gear`: the uuid of a gear item or (part of) its name from `get_gear`, both in one call to swap shoes. A name that fits several active items is refused before anything is changed. The distance and activity count of the gear are read again afterwards, so `get_gear` shows the change at once. Adding, editing and retiring gear is not there yet. ([#2](https://github.com/endurancetools/garmin-mcp/issues/2))

## 0.5.0 (2026-10-30)

### Activities

- **Running dynamics without the raw stream.** `get_activity` with the default `detail: "summary"` now returns the grade adjusted pace (`avg_gap_pace`), `running_dynamics` (cadence, ground contact time and its left/right balance, vertical oscillation, vertical ratio, stride length), `respiration` (average, min and max breaths per minute), `stamina` (start, end, lowest available), the Training Effect `label` and messages in `training_effect`, run/walk/stand time from Garmin's run-walk detection (`motion`) and where the heart rate came from (`hr_source`: `external_sensor` with the sensor's connection and battery in `hr_sensor`, or `watch_optical`; Garmin does not say whether a sensor is a chest strap or an arm band). Before, these were only in `raw_summary` of `detail: "full"`, next to the second-by-second stream. Fields the activity has no data for are left out.
- **Laps with running dynamics.** Each lap of `detail: "splits"` adds moving time, grade adjusted pace, normalized power, ground contact time and balance, vertical oscillation and ratio, stride length, breathing rate and elevation loss, so the first and last kilometres can be compared.
- **No personal data or GPS in the raw output by default.** `raw_summary` of `detail: "full"` leaves out your name, profile id and photo links, activity photos, device and sensor serial numbers, install ids, the start and end position and the activity's time zone, and the stream leaves out latitude and longitude. `GARMIN_REDACT=false` returns Garmin's data as sent. The data in the local cache is unchanged.
- **The stream aggregated on the server.** `get_activity` with `detail: "stream_summary"` and `bucket` (`km` by default, `5min`, `halves`, `quarters`) returns one row per bucket: distance, pace and grade adjusted pace (speed for other sports), average and max heart rate, power, cadence, ground contact time and balance, vertical oscillation and ratio, stride length, breathing rate, Performance Condition, elevation change and stamina at the end, with means weighted by time. `performance_condition` adds the first value (Garmin shows it after 6-20 minutes of running) with its time, the last one and the range. Halves or kilometres can be compared in one call of a few kilobytes instead of paging through the raw stream; the stream is downloaded once, like for `full`.
- **A thinner raw stream.** `detail: "full"` takes `metrics` (stream keys to keep, e.g. `["directHeartRate","directPower"]`; timestamp and timer time always stay) and `downsample_s` (at most one sample per that many seconds). `meta.note` names asked metrics the stream does not have.
- **Laps with workout steps.** Each lap of `detail: "splits"` has its `intensity` (`warmup`, `active`, `rest`, `cooldown`...; Garmin marks laps of a run without a workout as `interval` or `active`) and, for a workout, the step it was run in as `get_workout` lists it (`wkt_step`, e.g. `"3.1. Interval 100 m - Hill"`) with Garmin's score of how well the lap kept to the step's target (`compliance_pct`, 0-100; none for a step without a target or ending on the lap button). The overview has `workout_id` of the workout the activity followed and the score of the whole activity (`workout_compliance_pct`). Garmin numbers the steps of a lap (`wkt_step_index`, kept) as a FIT file does, with a repeat block counted after its steps, so the raw index does not match the step numbers; `wkt_step` does. The workout's steps are downloaded once and stored; `meta.note` says when the workout was changed after the activity or no longer exists.
- **Denser activity lists with filters.** `get_activities` adds the grade adjusted pace, run or bike cadence, normalized power, the Training Effect label and, when the activity's weather was downloaded, the air temperature and dew point (the list's own temperature is the watch's sensor, warmed by the wrist, and is not used). New filters: `min_duration_min`, `max_duration_min`, a `pace` range per km (`"4:45-5:30"`) and `name` (part of the name, any case, e.g. a reference route).
- `meta.fetched_at` is when the oldest record in an answer was downloaded; the server instructions now say so. A list with a session from today can still show an older time when other sessions on the page were fetched earlier.

### Training analytics

- **Compare sessions in one call.** New tool `compare_activities`: `ids` puts 2 to 10 activities side by side; `similar_to` finds the stored sessions like one activity (same type, duration within ±20 %, pace or GAP within ±20 s/km, optionally `same_route` and `easy_only`), newest first, each with how it differs (`match`). Every row has pace, GAP, heart rate, power, EF, decoupling, IF, TSS, the share in zones 4-5, cadence and running dynamics, and the weather when downloaded.
- **Running dynamics in `get_load`.** `include_activities` rows carry ground contact time and balance, vertical oscillation and ratio, stride length and breathing rate. `include_dynamics` adds the monthly running dynamics of road runs, also at a reference pace (`_at_ref`), from a regression on speed within months, so that a month of faster runs does not look like better form.
- **Pw:Hr for running.** In `get_load` with `include_activities`, runs with running power get `ef_power` (normalized power, else average power, per beat) and, when they qualify for decoupling, `decoupling_pw_pct` next to the Pa:Hr `decoupling_pct`. Every `decoupling_pct` names its basis in `decoupling_basis` (`pa_hr` or `pw_hr`); a run whose laps have power but no grade adjusted speed reports Pw:Hr there instead of nothing. `include_efficiency` adds `ef_power_avg` for running.

## 0.4.2 (2026-10-28)

### Activities

- **An activity deleted in Garmin Connect disappears from the local cache too.** Before, a sync only added and updated activities, so one deleted in Connect stayed in `get_activities` and the analytics until it was removed by hand. Now, once a sync has the whole activity list of a range, a stored activity of that range missing from the list is checked with Garmin and removed when Garmin answers 404 for it; any other answer keeps it, and a list that fails part way removes nothing. More than 20 such activities in one list are taken for a bad list and none is removed. The list of today and yesterday is fetched again when it is older than 15 minutes, so a recent deletion goes with the next read or sync; for an older one run `garmin sync --scope activities --from <date> --to <date> --force`. `garmin sync` and `garmin_sync` report the removals (`removed`). This mirrors your account and needs no `GARMIN_ENABLE_DESTRUCTIVE`: nothing is deleted in Garmin Connect.
- **`get_activity` on an activity deleted in Garmin Connect** removes it from the cache and says so, instead of a bare 404, when it has to ask Garmin for a section it has not stored yet.

### Races in the calendar

- **Calendar months that are not over yet go stale after 15 minutes instead of an hour,** like today's health data. A race added or moved in Garmin Connect shows up in `list_workouts` at most 15 minutes later, through the automatic sync; months fetched after they ended stay final.

## 0.4.1 (2026-10-16)

### Races in the calendar

- **Races from Garmin's event catalogue show up in `list_workouts`.** Garmin sends a race added from its event search with no calendar id, only an event uuid; such races were skipped, so `upcoming_events` missed them. Calendar events are now keyed by the calendar id or, without one, by the event uuid (database schema 7). The upgrade drops the list of cached calendar months, so the next `list_workouts` fetches them again.
- **A race added to a future month appears without a manual sync.** A calendar month that is not over yet goes stale after an hour, as before, but a read now treats it as missing too, so `list_workouts` refreshes it through the automatic sync (`GARMIN_AUTO_FETCH`). Before, a month fetched once was never fetched again by a read, and a race added later stayed invisible until `garmin sync --scope reference --force`.

## 0.3.0 (2026-10-13)

### Claude Desktop extension

- The extension is called **Garmin MCP** in Claude Desktop, not "Garmin Connect", which is Garmin's own service. Its description says it is an unofficial, open-source server, not affiliated with Garmin.
- Every release also carries the extension as `garmin-mcp.mcpb`, so `releases/latest/download/garmin-mcp.mcpb` always downloads the newest one.

### Morning readiness

- **REST needs two signals that agree.** By default (`decision_rule: "pairs"`) the decision is REST only when both signals of a pair are red: HRV with resting heart rate, HRV with sleep, or load with HRV, resting heart rate or sleep. A single red signal gives MODIFY. One short night lowers HRV, raises resting heart rate and shortens sleep together, so the old counting rule could cancel a key session on one event. `rules: {"decision_rule": "count"}` restores it.
- **The decision uses HRV, resting heart rate, sleep hours and load only.** Sleep is rated by hours; the sleep score stays in the answer. Body Battery and stress are shown for reference (`info`), like Garmin's Training Readiness, and do not vote. The rules `sleep_score_red`, `sleep_score_yellow`, `bb_red`, `bb_yellow` and `stress_yellow` are gone and are ignored with a note.

### Training load

- **Aerobic decoupling** (Pa:Hr for running, Pw:Hr for power) per session in `get_load` activities, and its monthly mean in the efficiency trend: the efficiency of the first and second half of a steady session of at least an hour, from the laps of the activity.
- **`flags.intensity_sports`** chooses which sport groups the intensity distribution counts (default run, bike, nordic, row). Add `hike` to include mountain walking; it then also gets an efficiency trend from grade adjusted speed.

### Documentation

- New pages: [Methods and evidence](https://endurancetools.github.io/garmin-mcp/methods/) (what fitness, fatigue, form and HRV readiness mean and where the methods come from) and an [FAQ](https://endurancetools.github.io/garmin-mcp/faq/).
- Every npm package has its own README; `SECURITY.md`, `CONTRIBUTING.md` and issue and pull request templates in the repository.

## 0.2.0 (2026-10-12)

### Training analytics

A new package, `garmin-mcp-analytics`, computes training metrics on your machine from the data already in the local database. Every formula, default and limit is described on the [Training analytics](https://endurancetools.github.io/garmin-mcp/analytics/) page.

- **`get_load`**: an own load model. Each activity gets one training stress from the first source available: power (cycling, TSS), pace (running, rTSS), swim pace against CSS (sTSS), heart rate (hrTSS from the time in HR zones, else from average heart rate) or session RPE. From it: fitness, fatigue and form (CTL, ATL, TSB), ACWR, monotony and strain, weekly volume per sport, a three-zone intensity split against LT1 and LTHR, load flags, an optional form per sport and a monthly efficiency factor trend over aerobic sessions.
- **`get_readiness`**: a morning GO, MODIFY or REST decision from overnight HRV, resting heart rate, sleep, Body Battery, stress and load, each compared with your own recent baseline. Garmin's Training Readiness is shown next to it for reference and does not vote.
- **`get_thresholds`**: LTHR, LT1, HRmax, FTP, resting heart rate and the other thresholds in effect on a date, with their history, per-sport values, and the heart rate and power zones of every sport in the Garmin profile.
- **`set_threshold`**: record a field test result. Every metric is computed with the thresholds that applied on the activity's own date, so a new test changes the numbers from its date on.
- Thresholds start from the Garmin profile, which is downloaded before an analytics read when it is not stored yet. Without a tested value, documented approximations are used and the answer says so.
- Every flag and readiness rule can be overridden per call (`flags`, `rules`), and every answer returns the values it used.

### CLI

- `garmin load`, `garmin readiness` and `garmin thresholds` (`list`, `add`, `export`, `import`) give the same results in the terminal, as Markdown or `--json`. They never download; a missing range is reported with the `garmin sync` command to run.
- `garmin import-activities <file.csv...>` reads the Activities CSV export of Garmin Connect (English or Polish headers), so load history older than the synced range counts too. Imported activities count only on dates the synced activities do not cover.
- `garmin status` shows the number of stored thresholds and imported activities.

### Changed

- **`preview_workout` is merged into `create_workout`.** Call `create_workout` with `dry_run: true` to see the readable steps and the estimated duration without saving anything; the same input without `dry_run` saves the workout. The tool count stays at 20.
- `get_training_status` and `get_period_summary`: the CTL/ATL/TSB computed from Garmin's per-activity training load is now one field, `garmin_load_model` (formerly `computed_load` and `load_at_end`), and the descriptions say when to use it and when `get_load`.
- The server (`serverInfo.version`) and `garmin --version` report the version from `package.json` instead of a fixed `0.1.0`.

### Release

- The Claude Desktop extension `garmin-mcp-<version>.mcpb` is attached to every GitHub release.
